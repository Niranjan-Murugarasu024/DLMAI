## 1. Theoretical Definition of Pharmaceutical Market Attractiveness

In commercial pharmaceutical resource allocation, a district is defined as **highly attractive** if it exhibits a potent confluence of:
1. **High Unmet Clinical Need:** Large prevalence of chronic, non-communicable, and acute diseases requiring ongoing pharmacotherapy.
2. **Substantial Market Scale & Urban Density:** Sufficient population base and urban concentration to support efficient medical representative (MR) routes, pharmacy distribution, and specialist doctor coverage.
3. **Established Clinical Access:** Physical primary and secondary healthcare facilities where diagnoses are rendered and prescriptions are generated.
4. **Demonstrated Purchasing Power:** Household economic capacity to finance healthcare expenditures through out-of-pocket spending or health insurance schemes.
5. **Robust Medicine Distribution Channels:** Density of retail pharmaceutical points (including Jan Aushadhi generic stores) facilitating physical medicine fulfillment.
6. **Moderate Competitive Saturation:** Favorable balance between pharmaceutical corporate presence and untapped patient demand.

## 3. Construct Validation Questions for Each Pillar

### Pillar 1: Healthcare Need
- **What it measures:** Burden of chronic diseases (Hypertension, Diabetes) and child malnutrition (Stunting, Wasting).
- **Why it matters:** Direct driver of prescription therapy volumes (cardiovascular, metabolic, nutritional).
- **Evidence type:** Direct observed epidemiology from NFHS-5 clinical biomarker measurements.

### Pillar 2: Demographic Market Potential
- **What it measures:** Aggregate patient pool and urbanization density.
- **Why it matters:** Determines total addressable market (TAM) and logistical field-force efficiency.
- **Evidence type:** Census 2011 PCA population and urban proportions.

### Pillar 3: Healthcare Access & Inpatient Capacity
- **What it measures:** Public primary and secondary care infrastructure per 100k population.
- **Why it matters:** Facilities where formal medical consultations, diagnostics, and prescription generation occur.
- **Evidence type:** MoHFW Rural Health Statistics administrative records.

### Pillar 4: Healthcare Utilization
- **What it measures:** Rate at which the local population seeks formal medical care (institutional deliveries, antenatal visits).
- **Why it matters:** Differentiates latent need from active healthcare consumption behavior.
- **Evidence type:** NFHS-5 maternal-child health utilization records.

### Pillar 5: Economic Affordability & Purchasing Capacity
- **What it measures:** Ability to afford out-of-pocket medical treatments and insurance risk pooling.
- **Why it matters:** Ensures patients can fulfill prescriptions for branded or private-market medicines.
- **Evidence type:** NFHS-5 out-of-pocket delivery spending and household health insurance penetration.

### Pillar 6: Medicine Availability & Retail Access
- **What it measures:** Geographic density of physical pharmacy distribution points.
- **Why it matters:** Ensures physical fulfillment and retail accessibility of medicines.
- **Evidence type:** PMBJP Jan Aushadhi retail Kendra registry (5,926 South India outlets).

### Pillar 7: Pharmaceutical Corporate & Manufacturing Concentration
- **What it measures:** Registered active pharmaceutical manufacturing and corporate headquarters.
- **Why it matters:** Identifies macro industrial clustering, supplier density, and local corporate saturation.
- **Evidence type:** MCA21 corporate master registry (**943,267 companies, 21,707 pharma records**).

### Pillar 8: Market Expansion & Policy Priority
- **What it measures:** Accelerated government development focus and demographic growth trajectory.
- **Why it matters:** Identifies high-growth expansion territories benefiting from public health investments.
- **Evidence type:** NITI Aayog Aspirational Programme registry.
